\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

This chapter provides the main tips and guidelines for writing theses and reports using \LaTeX.

\section{What Makes a Good Report}
\label{sec:c1:goodreport}

Regardless of the type of project, a good report reads as a reflective account of the work as a whole, not just a walkthrough of what was built or done. Keep the following in mind:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Separate technical \textbf{description} (what was done) from technical \textbf{analysis} (why, and what it means).
    \item Justify non-trivial decisions against the alternatives you considered, rather than just listing what you picked.
    \item Include a dedicated discussion of your results and outcomes: what worked, what did not, and why.
    \item Close with an honest reflection that revisits your goals individually, not a single rounding-up sentence.
    \item Use references where relevant (methods, design patterns, related work); citation discipline still matters outside a thesis.
    \item Keep the body lean; move supporting material (test logs, specifications, mockups, extra data) to the appendices.
\end{itemize}

\section{Thesis Structure}
\label{sec:c1:thesesguidelines}

You should shop around and look at theses or ask your supervisor for suggestions.
That said, here is an example of how you can structure your thesis.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Introduction
    \item Background / Related Work
    \item Study Design / Methodology
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Objective and Research Questions
        \item Data Collection
        \item Data Analysis
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Results
    \item Discussion
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Interpretation of Results
        \item Implications to Practitioners and Researchers
        \item Threats to Validity
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Conclusions
\end{enumerate}

\section{Internship Structure}
\label{sec:c1:internshipguidelines}

Whether you take the Bachelor Internship (5 or 15 EC) or the Master's In-company or Research Internship (15 EC), you will do it either \textbf{with a research group} or \textbf{in-company}. That aspect is what should drive the report's structure; the degree and number of credits affect the expected depth and scope.

\subsection{Research-hosted Internship}
\label{sec:c1:researchinternship}

A research-hosted internship comprises work similar to a thesis but at a smaller scale. Thus, follow the thesis structure in \autoref{sec:c1:thesesguidelines}, scaled to the internship's smaller scope.

If the internship is of a more practical nature, e.g., implementing a tool for a piece of research, a structure similar to that of a company-hosted internship (see below) might be more meaningful. In such a case, consult your supervisor.

\subsection{Company-hosted Internship}
\label{sec:c1:companyinternship}

A company-hosted internship is not a research project, so forcing it into the thesis structure above does not work well. Consider instead:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Introduction
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Host Organization
        \item Your Role and Responsibilities
        \item Learning Goals
        \item Skills Developed (technical, professional, personal)
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Methodology / Approach
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Planned vs. Actual Timeline
        \item Team Process (meetings, delivery strategy) and its Rationale
        \item Your Role within the Team
        \item Technical Decisions, Justified against Alternatives
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Results\\
    It varies per project; some examples include:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Architecture and Design Decisions
        \item Technical Work Carried Out
        \item Testing Evidence (e.g. number of tests, coverage, pass rate)
        \item Results and Outcomes
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Conclusion
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Learning Goals Revisited, with Evidence
        \item Unexpected Lessons
        \item Future Work
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\section{\LaTeX{} Formatting}
\label{sec:c1:latex}

There are some great resources to help with \LaTeX,
so this guide will not include general instructions.
For those, consult the Latex Wikibook\footnote{\url{https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/LaTeX}}
and the excellent guidelines by Spinellis~\cite{spinellis:latexadvice}.

That said, there are some small pointers that are particular to this template.
These are mentioned in the subsection below.

\subsection{References}
\label{sec:c1:References}

\noindent
\textbf{Indirect citation}

These look like ``\verb|... in related work~\cite{source}|'' and generate something like ``\dots in related work~[2].''
Do not forget the ``\verb|~|'' before ``\verb|\cite|'' to not let the next line start with ``\verb|[2]|''.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\textbf{Direct citation}

These look like ``\texttt{according to Author et} \verb|al.~\cite{source}|'' and generate something like ``according to Author et al. [2].''
Only add ``et al.'' if the reference is by three or more people.

\subsection{Tables}
\label{sec:c1:latex_tables}

Avoid cumbersome table formatting (e.g. using vertical lines).
In general, simpler is better.
\autoref{tab:c1:simpleexample} and \autoref{tab:c1:multicolexample} are some examples.
Also, look at the tables' source for code formatting tips.

\begin{table} [h]
    \caption{Example of a simple table (based on~\cite{spinellis:latexadvice})}
    \label{tab:c1:simpleexample}
    
    \noindent
    \centering
    \footnotesize%if needed

    % put the header on a \parbox to center and break lines
    \newcommand{\hb}[2]{\parbox[c][0.8cm][c]{#1}{\centering #2}}
    
    \begin{tabular}{lccccc}
        \toprule
        % Column labels (using separate lines to align label with data)
        \hb{2cm}{Command\\and options}
                & \hb{2cm}{Input\\requirements}
                        & Matched
                                & Connected
                                        & Matched
                                                & Connected \\
        \midrule
        tr -cs  & 1     & \X    & --    & \V    & 1 \\
        sort w  & 0     &       & --    & \X    & 0 \\
        fmt     & 1     & \X    & --    & \V    & 1 \\
        tr A-Z  & 1     & \V    & 1     &       & 1 \\
        sort -u & fmt   & \X    & --    & \V    & 1 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Figures}
\label{sec:c1:latex_figures}

The main tips in a nutshell are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Put all your figures inside the \texttt{figs} folder,
          and use subfolders to organize them per chapter.
    \item Prefer vectorized images (e.g. PS or PDF).
          Bear in mind that saving a PNG or JPG as PDF
          will not vectorize your image.
\end{itemize}

See \autoref{fig:c1:simpleexample} for a simple example.
